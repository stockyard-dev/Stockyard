Self-Hosted Notion Alternative

Notes, wikis, and project management without per-seat pricing or your team's knowledge living on Notion's servers.

What teams actually use Notion for

Notion is a workspace that does many things: notes, wikis, project management, databases, and documents. At $8 per user per month (Plus plan), a 25-person team pays $200/month. But most teams use Notion for two or three of its capabilities, not all of them. A team that uses Notion primarily as an internal wiki and a task board is paying for a general-purpose workspace when focused tools would cost less and work better for each specific use case.

Focused replacements

Handbook — replaces Notion as an internal wiki. Create pages organized by space (engineering, operations, onboarding), full-text search, version history. The core wiki experience without the block editor complexity. $7.99/mo.

Notebook — replaces Notion for personal notes and documents. Create, organize, and search notes. Markdown support, fast full-text search. For personal knowledge management rather than team wikis. $7.99/mo.

Prairie — replaces Notion's kanban board view. Custom columns, cards with labels and assignees, drag-and-drop. The visual project management that teams use Notion boards for. $7.99/mo.

Bounty — replaces Notion's issue tracking databases. Bug tracker with priorities, assignees, milestones, and labels. For teams that built issue tracking in Notion databases. $7.99/mo.

When to stay on Notion

Notion's strength is the unified workspace — notes, databases, and project management in one tool with cross-linking between them. If your team heavily uses Notion databases with relations, rollups, and formulas, or if the block editor is central to how your team creates documents, no collection of focused tools replicates that integrated experience. Self-hosted alternatives make sense when you use Notion for specific, separable workflows that do not depend on cross-database relations.
