Books vs Wave

Wave is Free (ad-supported). Books is a self-hosted alternative at $0.99/mo. Here's when each makes sense.

Pick Books when

Pick Books when you want simplicity and ownership.

  • Self-hosted — financial data stays private
  • No ads, no data mining
  • Double-entry bookkeeping
  • No transaction fees

Books is a single Go binary with embedded SQLite. Install it with one command, and you are running in under a minute. Your data stays on your server.

curl -fsSL https://stockyard.dev/books/install.sh | sh

Books overview

Wave strengths

Wave makes sense when you need more.

  • Free with full accounting features
  • Invoice sending and payment processing
  • Receipt scanning
  • Bank connections and auto-import
The trade-off

Choosing between Books and Wave is about infrastructure preferences. Wave at Free (ad-supported) handles hosting for you. Books at $0.99/mo puts the control — and the data — in your hands.

Migration path

The migration from Wave to Books is straightforward for most teams. Export your data, POST each record to the API. A migration script is typically fewer than 50 lines.

FAQ
Is Books a full accounting system?
Books handles double-entry bookkeeping with accounts, transactions, and P&L reports. For payroll, tax filing, and bank connections, Wave or QuickBooks offers more.
Can Books import from QuickBooks or Wave?
Books accepts transactions through its API. Export from your current system as CSV and POST each transaction to the Books API.
