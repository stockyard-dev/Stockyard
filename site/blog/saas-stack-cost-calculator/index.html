How much does your SaaS stack actually cost?

I added up every SaaS tool our team was paying for. The number was worse than I expected.

I went through our credit card statement last month and pulled out every recurring charge for developer tools. Not counting infrastructure (AWS, hosting) — just the tools that help us build, ship, and operate software.

Here is what a typical 5-person engineering team pays:

ToolWhat it doesMonthly cost
Statuspage.ioStatus page$79
LaunchDarkly (5 seats)Feature flags$50
Sentry TeamError tracking$26
Bitly GrowthLink shortening$35
MixpanelAnalytics$20
TypeformForms$25
Toggl (5 seats)Time tracking$45
FreshBooksInvoicing$15
1Password Teams (5)Secrets$20
HookdeckWebhook testing$25
Total$340/mo ($4,080/yr)

Four thousand dollars a year on tools. Not on servers, not on employees, not on marketing — on SaaS subscriptions for features that most of these tools implement as a few thousand lines of code with a database behind them.

The worst part is the per-seat pricing. LaunchDarkly charges $10/seat/mo. Toggl charges $9/seat/mo. Add one engineer and your bill goes up $19/mo across just those two tools. Scale to 20 engineers and you are paying $380/mo for feature flags and time tracking alone.

$340/mo to $12/mo. Or $29/mo for Stockyard Complete, which includes all 150 tools.

The tradeoff is real

Self-hosted means you are running the tools. If the server goes down, you fix it. If there is a bug, you wait for an update or work around it. You do not get a support team or an SLA.

The tools are also simpler than their SaaS counterparts. Seismograph does not have Sentry's performance monitoring. Salt Lick does not have LaunchDarkly's 25 SDKs. Headcount does not have Mixpanel's cohort analysis.

For most small teams, the core features are enough. If you need enterprise capabilities, the SaaS tools exist for a reason.

But if you are paying $4,000/yr for tools you use 20% of, the math is hard to ignore.
