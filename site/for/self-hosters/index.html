For Self-Hosters

Your server. Your data. Your rules.

Stockyard runs on your infrastructure. One binary, one SQLite file, Docker-ready. No SaaS dependency, no data leaving your network, no usage-based pricing for the platform itself.

Why self-host your LLM proxy

Managed LLM gateways like Helicone and Portkey are good products, but they add a dependency. Your prompts and responses pass through their servers. Your usage data lives on their infrastructure. If they have an outage, your proxy is down.

Self-hosting means your LLM traffic stays on your network. The only external calls are to the LLM providers you configure (OpenAI, Anthropic, etc.). Stockyard itself does not phone home, does not collect telemetry, and does not require an internet connection beyond reaching your providers.

Deploy your way

Bare metal or VM: Download the binary, run it. Works on any Linux machine.

curl -fsSL stockyard.dev/install.sh | sh export OPENAI_API_KEY=sk-your-key stockyard # Stockyard running on :7749

Docker: One container, one volume for the SQLite database. See the Docker guide for docker-compose.yml examples including reverse proxy and OTEL sidecar setups.

Railway, Fly.io, or any container platform: Stockyard deploys anywhere a Docker container runs. We run on Railway ourselves. See the Railway guide.

What you control

Data residency. Traces, costs, and audit logs never leave your server. The SQLite file is the entire database.

Updates. You choose when to upgrade. Download a new binary when you are ready, not when a SaaS provider pushes a change.

Backups. Copy one file. No managed backup service, no snapshot scheduler.

Network. Monitor exactly what leaves your server. Stockyard only connects to the LLM providers you configure. No telemetry, no analytics.
