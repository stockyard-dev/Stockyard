Bellwether vs UptimeRobot

UptimeRobot is $7/mo (Pro). Bellwether is a self-hosted alternative at $0.99/mo. Here's when each makes sense.

Pick Bellwether when

Pick Bellwether when you want simplicity and ownership.

  • Self-hosted — monitor from inside your network
  • HTTP, TCP, and DNS checks
  • No data sent to third party
  • $0.99/mo vs $7/mo

Bellwether is a single Go binary with embedded SQLite. Install it with one command, and you are running in under a minute. Your data stays on your server.

UptimeRobot strengths

UptimeRobot makes sense when you need more.

  • 50 free monitors
  • SMS and phone call alerts
  • Status pages included
  • Multi-location monitoring
Making the decision

Teams evaluating these two options split on feature depth versus data residency. UptimeRobot has more features. Bellwether keeps your data on your server in a SQLite file you can inspect and back up without asking permission.

Switching from UptimeRobot

Try Bellwether in parallel first. The data directory is isolated — a trial costs nothing beyond compute. If it fits, bring your data over via the API.

FAQ
Can Bellwether monitor from multiple locations?
Bellwether runs from one location — your server. For multi-location external monitoring, UptimeRobot or similar services complement it well.
Does Bellwether send alerts?
Bellwether tracks incidents and status changes. Pair it with a webhook to send alerts to Slack, PagerDuty, or email.
Technical differences

Bellwether runs as a single static binary — one file, one process, one SQLite database. There is no application server to configure, no cache layer to tune, no background worker to monitor. UptimeRobot runs on distributed cloud infrastructure with managed databases, CDNs, and multi-region redundancy. The tradeoff is clear: UptimeRobot handles more complexity out of the box, while Bellwether handles the same core workflow with dramatically less operational surface area.

What you keep

The exit story matters more than most teams realize when choosing tools. UptimeRobot locks your data behind their API and export format. Bellwether stores everything in a standard SQLite file you can query with any database tool. If you outgrow Bellwether, your data migrates with a SQL query. If you outgrow UptimeRobot, your data migrates through whatever export process they provide — which may or may not preserve everything.

The right time to move

Teams typically switch from UptimeRobot to Bellwether for one of three reasons: the bill got too high, a compliance requirement mandated self-hosting, or the team simply wanted more control over their uptime and health monitor data. Whatever the trigger, the migration itself is mechanical — export data from UptimeRobot, import via Bellwether's API. The harder part is building the habit of using a new tool. Give your team two weeks to adjust before drawing conclusions.
